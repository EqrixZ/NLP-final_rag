# Chili Anthracnose (โรคแอนแทรคโนสพริก / โรคกุ้งแห้ง)

## Overview
Anthracnose is a major fruit disease of chili and sweet pepper in tropical Asia, including Thailand. In Thai it is commonly called "โรคกุ้งแห้ง" (literally "dried shrimp disease") because badly infected chili pods bend, shrivel and twist like dried shrimp. Losses of marketable fruit of roughly 10–80% have been reported in Asian countries.

## Cause
The disease is caused by several fungi in the genus *Colletotrichum*, mainly *C. acutatum*, *C. capsici* (also listed as *C. truncatum*) and *C. gloeosporioides*. *C. acutatum* is considered the most aggressive and can produce symptoms on fruit earlier than the other species. Their wide host range (e.g. tomato, eggplant, papaya, mango) means other crops and weeds can harbor the fungus.

## Symptoms
- **Fruit:** Small, slightly sunken spots appear, usually as fruit ripens, then enlarge into round or oval sunken lesions that may merge in wet weather.
- **Spore masses:** In humid weather, pink to orange slimy spore masses form on lesions in concentric rings; tiny black fungal structures develop later.
- **Fruit distortion:** Severely infected pods become curved, shriveled and deformed, rot, and may drop before harvest.
- **Leaves and stems:** Irregular brown spots with darker borders can occur, but leaf symptoms are much less common than fruit symptoms.

## Favorable Conditions
- Warm, wet weather; the Pacific Pests fact sheet cites temperatures around 27 °C with humidity above 80%.
- Long periods of leaf and fruit wetness from rain, dew or overhead irrigation.
- Spread: spores move mainly by rain and irrigation splash, also by wind and insects. The fungus survives on crop debris, weeds and other hosts, and is seed-borne.

## Management
### Clean seed and seedlings
- Buy seed and seedlings from disease-free sources; if saving seed, take it only from healthy fruit.
- Hot-water seed treatment: the Thai Department of Agricultural Extension advises soaking seed in water at about 50 °C for 20–25 minutes, while the Pacific Pests fact sheet gives 52 °C for 30 minutes. Control temperature and time precisely.

### Cultural practices
- Use adequate plant spacing and control weeds to improve air flow and reduce humidity.
- Avoid overhead irrigation; if it must be used, water early in the day so plants dry before evening.
- Scout fields regularly, especially from flowering to harvest. Pick and destroy infected fruit away from the field.
- Remove or bury crop residues after harvest.
- Rotate with non-solanaceous crops where disease has been severe.
- Use resistant or tolerant varieties where available (e.g. lines bred by the World Vegetable Center/AVRDC), checking local performance.

### Chemical control (active-ingredient level)
- Protective fungicides such as mancozeb or copper compounds, applied from flowering onward when weather favors disease.
- Thai official recommendations also list systemic actives such as prochloraz, azoxystrobin and difenoconazole, sprayed at about 5–7 day intervals when the disease appears.
- Rotate fungicides with different modes of action to slow resistance, and follow the label rate and pre-harvest interval.

## Precautions
- Read and follow the label, wear protective equipment, and respect pre-harvest intervals, as chili is often eaten fresh.
- Consult local agricultural extension officers (เจ้าหน้าที่ส่งเสริมการเกษตร) for current recommendations, suitable varieties and correct diagnosis.

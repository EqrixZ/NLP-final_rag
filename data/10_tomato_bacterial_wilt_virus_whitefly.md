# Tomato and Solanaceous Vegetables: Bacterial Wilt, Leaf Curl Virus, Whitefly and Thrips

## Bacterial wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum)
Bacterial wilt is one of the most damaging soil-borne diseases of tomato, chilli, eggplant and potato in the tropics, including Thailand. The bacterium also attacks ginger-family crops such as fingerroot (krachai).

### Symptoms
- Young leaves wilt first, then the whole plant collapses quickly, often while leaves are still green (little yellowing or spotting).
- Plants may look better at night or early morning, then wilt again in the heat of the day.
- Cutting the lower stem shows brown discoloured vascular tissue; the pith may become dark and soft.

### Stem-in-water ooze test (field diagnosis)
1. Cut a clean section of the lower stem from a freshly wilted plant.
2. Hang it in a clear glass of water so the cut end just touches the water.
3. Within about 3–5 minutes, milky white threads of bacteria and slime stream from the cut end; in severe infections the water turns cloudy in 10–15 minutes.
A positive stream strongly suggests bacterial wilt. No stream does not rule it out; wilting from Fusarium, root rot or drought gives no milky ooze.

### Management
- No chemical cures an infected plant. Pull out wilted plants with their roots and remove them from the field.
- Rotate away from solanaceous crops; avoid planting where wilt occurred before.
- Use resistant varieties. World Vegetable Center research found that grafting tomato onto wilt-resistant eggplant rootstocks lowered wilting and raised yield.
- Improve drainage and avoid moving infested soil or water between plots. Clean tools and avoid wounding roots.
- DOA field work in fingerroot combined pre-plant soil treatment with urea and lime and a Bacillus subtilis bioproduct (strain BS-DOA 24), which reduced wilt. Ask extension officers for local recommendations.

## Tomato yellow leaf curl virus (TYLCV) and whitefly
TYLCV belongs to the begomoviruses spread by the sweetpotato whitefly, Bemisia tabaci. Related leaf curl begomoviruses also occur in Thailand.

### Symptoms
- Stunted plants with small, upward-cupped leaves and bright yellow leaf margins.
- Few flowers and fruit. Early infection causes the greatest yield loss.

### Whitefly biology
- Tiny white adults on leaf undersides. Egg to adult takes about three weeks in warm weather.
- Once a whitefly picks up TYLCV it can transmit the virus for life (persistent transmission).
- Feeding also produces honeydew and sooty mould.

### Management
- Start with virus-free, whitefly-free transplants. Raise seedlings under insect-proof net.
- Plant tolerant or resistant varieties where available.
- Reflective (metallic) plastic mulch repels adult whiteflies early in the season.
- Destroy crop residues promptly after harvest and remove weed hosts. A host-free gap breaks the whitefly–virus cycle.
- Remove infected plants early; they cannot be cured.
- If insecticides are needed, rotate modes of action. UF/IFAS suggests rotating about every five weeks, roughly one whitefly generation. Whiteflies develop resistance easily.

## Thrips
Thrips (for example Thrips palmi, and western flower thrips elsewhere) rasp leaves, flowers and fruit. The damage shows as silvery scarring, distorted or curled young leaves and scarred fruit. Some thrips spread tospoviruses such as Tomato spotted wilt virus. Thrips pick up the virus as larvae and can transmit it as adults. Tactics include resistant varieties, early-season thrips control, weed removal, blue or yellow sticky traps for monitoring, and removing infected plants.

## Safety note
Always identify the problem before treating. Use only registered products and read the label. Consult DOA or DOAE extension officers or a plant clinic for locally approved options.
